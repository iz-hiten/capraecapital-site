Brooklyn movie theaters discount tickets through tech startup

nydailynews.com Source: nydailynews.com ↗
Read the original at nydailynews.com ↗

THIS could take the financial bite out of movie night.

A Los Angeles-based tech company is billing itself as the Priceline of movie tickets, signing up Brooklyn theaters to unload their unsold seats at a discount.

The result: a cheaper night out for movie lovers and a better bottom line for theater owners, the company claims.

“Not only are our customers going to theater more, they’re also spending more on concessions,” Dealflicks co-founder Kevin Hong told the Daily News.

Theaters typically have stunningly high vacancy rates — 88% and 95% after a show’s opening weekend, Hong says — and Dealflicks works with theaters to offer some of those unused tickets for discounts of as much as 60%.

Movie watchers go to the company’s Website, find the theater and show they want to see and buy their tickets online.

Dealflicks has enlisted nearly 400 theaters across the country to use the service.

Four Brooklyn cinemas have signed on in the past year, and Hong said he’s negotiating with others in hopes of expanding in the borough.

“What we like about the Brooklyn market is we have a good number of small, independent theaters,” said Hong, who has made two trips to Kings County to make his sales pitch.

“It captures a more diverse market.”

The theaters determine the parameters of their deals, often bundling tickets with popcorn or soda as part of the discount.

Brooklyn Heights Cinema on Henry St. was one of the first in the borough to join Dealflicks.

The cinema’s owner, Kenn Lowy, sells as many as 50 extra seats a month through the site.

The customers are generally one-time visitors, drawn only by the deal, but it’s still extra business, Lowy said.

“It’s definitely 50 people who would not have shown up,” he said.

He compares the service to Groupon but says Dealflicks’ 15% fee is a fraction of what the discount giant wanted to charge him.

“The folks at Dealflicks want to get their foot in the door,” Lowy said.

“But they also don’t want to rip people off.”
