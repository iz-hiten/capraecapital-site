Web startup Dealflicks launches in Santa Cruz, hopes to bring youth back to the movies

santacruzsentinel.com Source: santacruzsentinel.com ↗
Read the original at santacruzsentinel.com ↗

SANTA CRUZ – Downtown moviegoers are about to save some cash starting this summer.

Prospective savings should profit Santa Cruzans as soon as web-based startup Dealflicks and theaters Nickelodeon and Del Mar sew up their agreement, in upcoming weeks.

Launched just two months ago in a partner theater in Los Angeles, Dealflicks’ deals are targeting students and young audiences, offering them daily movie tickets and concessions up to 50 percent off.

“We’re shooting for a launch in Santa Cruz before July 15,” said co-founder of Dealflicks Kevin Hong. “The Del Mar and the Nick were an incredible fit. They’re downtown, where everybody’s walking around and they’re only three miles away from campus; you can take a bus to get there.”

Having a college such as UCSC, neighboring downtown’s theaters is a key element to the launch, Hong said. Dealflicks, an Oakland-based startup operating in beta mode, has come up with a business model specially intended for younger audiences that have been abandoning movie theaters for Hulu or Netflix subscriptions.

The model’s cornerstone relies on a rather simple secret: Customers visiting Dealflicks’ website can only specify what neighborhood and day they want to watch a particular movie. Exact location and time are details provided once the ticket is purchased. While the scheme might not appeal to older well-off working audiences whose time is valuable, it fits college students’ needs, Hong said.

“It’s successful because it brings back the college demographics that have been walking away from theaters for the past few years,” Hong said. “College students have more free time and to them, even $3 or $4 matter. They’re the ones who want to go the extra mile to find a cheap theater they can go with their buddies.”

At 27, Hong is convinced the younger audience still enjoys going to the movies.

“Watching a movie in a theater is a whole experience,” Hong said. “You can’t invite a date to your house and say ‘let’s watch a movie.’ That doesn’t make any sense.”

But the price of movie tickets has become an issue, Hong said. Ticket prices in the U.S. hit an all-time high in 2011, reaching an average of $7.93, according to a report released by the Hollywood Reporter by the National Association of Theater Owners, released in February.

Dealflicks’ model is a win-win, Hong said. Partner theaters fill out seats that may have remained empty, customers dish out less cash, and the growing startup pockets 5 to 10 percent of each ticket sale. With such a revenue share model, Dealflicks’ CEO Sean Wycliffe and his co-founder hope to revolutionize the theater industry, Hong said.

Downtown Santa Cruz, Nickelodeon Theatres, the company owning both the Del Mar and the Nickelodeon, is willing to try the parternship. Chief Operating Officer Scott Griffin remains more cautious about Dealflicks’ chances of success.

“For us, it’s just a new kind of way to get exposure for the movies we’re playing,” Griffin said. “We’re invested in it as long as it works.”

The Santa Cruz group is committed to “demystify the art house”, Griffin said. With a film selection mostly favoring independent cinema in both theaters, fans of commercial blockbusters sometimes need incentives to push the door.

“People often end up realizing our films are really good once they come out on DVD, because they don’t want to take chances,” Griffin said. “But if they come and see one film that’s good, they’ll probably return and see more.”

The Del Mar and Nickelon will propose different kinds of offers through Dealflicks, all reducing the price on concession packages, Hong said.

“There is no ‘ticket only’ deal because there’s a minimum price we’re required to charge from distributors,” Griffin said.

With summer just around the corner, UCSC students sticking around Santa Cruz will have plenty of occasions to try the online service. A new philosophy graduate at UCSC, Mike Titcomb goes the movies once a month, he said. Told about Dealflicks’ arrival while checking times for “Moonrise Kingdom” on Tuesday, Titcomb was pleased with the announcement.

“I could definitely see myself using that service occasionally,” Titcomb said. “I have enough free time.”
